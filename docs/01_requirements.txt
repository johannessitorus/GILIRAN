GILIRAN
Employee Shift Swap & Handover Management System
DOCUMENT 01 — SOFTWARE REQUIREMENTS SPECIFICATION (SRS)
Version: 1.0 (MVP baseline)
Status: Requirements baseline for design; not implemented

1. PROJECT OVERVIEW
GILIRAN is a responsive web application for teams working in scheduled shifts. It combines two core workflows: employee-to-employee shift swaps, approved by a supervisor, and structured work handovers between consecutive shifts. It is intended as a full-stack portfolio project and a configurable foundation for organizations operating in shifts.

2. PROBLEM STATEMENT
- Swap requests handled through private chats are difficult to trace and approve consistently.
- Schedule edits can introduce overlaps, insufficient rest, or unqualified assignments.
- Incoming shifts can miss operational issues and unfinished tasks.
- Managers lack a clear audit history of who changed or acknowledged shift-related records.

3. PROJECT OBJECTIVES
OBJ-01 Centralize employee assignments and shift-swap requests.
OBJ-02 Require peer acceptance and supervisor approval before changing a schedule.
OBJ-03 Detect scheduling conflicts and enforce configured organizational rules.
OBJ-04 Record structured handover notes and unfinished tasks between shifts.
OBJ-05 Retain traceable decisions and schedule changes.
OBJ-06 Offer a responsive interface for employees, supervisors, and administrators.

4. USERS AND ACCESS CONTROL
4.1 Employee
- View own assignments and permitted team calendar entries.
- Request a two-way swap involving an assignment they own.
- Accept/reject swap requests addressed to them.
- Write and submit a handover for their assigned shift; acknowledge a handover when they own the designated recipient assignment.
- View own requests, assigned handovers, and relevant open tasks.

4.2 Supervisor
- View and maintain shift assignments for managed departments/teams.
- Review peer-accepted swap requests and approve/reject them.
- Review handovers and outstanding tasks within their responsibility.
- View scoped history and audit records.

4.3 Admin
- Manage accounts, employee-to-department membership, roles, shift types, qualifications, and system rules.
- Review organization-wide records and audit history.
- Admin privileges do not bypass audit logging or scheduling integrity checks.

Authorization: every read/write operation must enforce role, team scope, and ownership on the backend. Frontend hiding alone is insufficient.

5. FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS
FR-01 Authentication: login, logout, authenticated session, and password-protected accounts.
FR-02 User Management: admin creates/updates/deactivates users, assigns a role and department; activated employees must have valid team membership.
FR-03 Shift Scheduling: supervisors create/update assignments within managed departments, subject to validity and overlap rules.
FR-04 Shift Visibility: employees view their own assignments and a permitted team calendar; sensitive employee details are scoped.
FR-05 Shift Swap Request: employee selects their own future active assignment and another employee's eligible future assignment to propose a direct two-way exchange; records request and reason.
FR-06 Peer Decision: targeted employee accepts or rejects a pending request; actions are authenticated, scoped and idempotently protected against double processing.
FR-07 Supervisor Decision: authorized supervisor approves or rejects a peer-accepted request; approval applies the exchange atomically.
FR-08 Conflict Detection: validate ownership, overlapping assignments, minimum rest, relevant shift qualification, status, and competing active requests; revalidate at approval.
FR-09 Handover Authoring: outgoing assigned employee saves draft and submits handover notes, issues, and relevant context for a defined incoming assignment.
FR-10 Handover Acknowledgement: designated incoming employee acknowledges a submitted handover; time and actor are recorded.
FR-11 Task Tracking: handover report contains open/in-progress/done follow-up tasks visible to relevant staff; task changes are permission-checked.
FR-12 Audit Trail: important assignment, swap, and handover actions are appended with timestamp, actor, affected entity, and change summary.

6. NON-FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS
NFR-01 Security: hash passwords; authenticated API; server-side RBAC and ownership authorization; protect against mass assignment and inappropriate data exposure.
NFR-02 Integrity: swap approval uses one database transaction; either both assignments, request status, and audit record update, or none do.
NFR-03 Usability: responsive desktop/mobile experience; status and decision history are understandable.
NFR-04 Performance: initial target for normal API reads is under 1 second in an agreed local test workload; actual performance must be measured, not assumed.
NFR-05 Reliability: retries or concurrent actions cannot apply the same swap twice.
NFR-06 Auditability: capture who acted, what changed and when; historical records are not silently rewritten.
NFR-07 Maintainability: separate frontend, API, business services and persistence.
NFR-08 Testability: automate key validation and status-transition scenarios; use isolated test fixtures.
NFR-09 Time handling: store scheduled start/end as timezone-aware instants; display in configured organizational timezone.
NFR-10 Privacy: minimize employee personal data; apply departmental access boundaries.

7. SHIFT SWAP WORKFLOW
7.1 Preconditions
- Both participating employees are active and eligible.
- Both shift assignments exist, are distinct, active, and have not started.
- Requester owns the source; target employee owns the target.
- No active swap request currently reserves either assignment.
7.2 Statuses
PENDING_PEER -> PENDING_APPROVAL -> APPROVED.
Alternate terminal results: REJECTED, CANCELLED, EXPIRED.
7.3 Main flow
1) Employee A proposes a two-way swap of their assignment with Employee B's assignment.
2) System validates eligibility and persists request as PENDING_PEER.
3) Employee B accepts; status becomes PENDING_APPROVAL; or rejects -> REJECTED.
4) An authorized supervisor revalidates current assignments, conflicts, rest and qualifications.
5) Approval exchanges ownership atomically and sets APPROVED; rejection sets REJECTED.
6) Record decision and affected assignment changes in immutable history.
7.4 Exceptions
- Requester may cancel while pending; request expiry is enforced before actions.
- Changed/inactive/started assignments or conflicting concurrent requests block approval with a clear reason; no partial exchange is allowed.
- Repeated decisions must not apply the swap more than once.

8. SHIFT HANDOVER WORKFLOW
8.1 Preconditions
- An outgoing assignment and an identified next/recipient assignment are defined for the same operating context (team or work area).
- Incoming assignee is determined from the recipient assignment at acknowledgement time.
8.2 Statuses
DRAFT -> SUBMITTED -> ACKNOWLEDGED.
Task statuses: OPEN, IN_PROGRESS, DONE. Acknowledging a report does not mark tasks DONE.
8.3 Main flow
1) Outgoing assigned employee creates a handover draft for their shift.
2) Draft contains summary, issues, and follow-up tasks (if any).
3) Outgoing employee submits the report; submission actor and time are saved.
4) Current assignee of the designated recipient shift reviews and acknowledges it.
5) System records acknowledgement, while tasks remain independently trackable.
8.4 Exceptions
- An unauthorized employee cannot submit or acknowledge.
- A submitted report cannot be silently overwritten; corrections require an auditable revision process (deferred to design decision).
- A recipient assignment whose owner changes due to a swap must be rechecked when acknowledgement occurs.

9. BUSINESS RULES
BR-01 Only a currently assigned employee may request a swap of their active assignment.
BR-02 Both assignments must be future and not yet started at creation and final approval.
BR-03 Only the targeted employee can accept or reject the peer request.
BR-04 Supervisor approval is required before any actual schedule exchange.
BR-05 Assignments of the same employee must not overlap; overlap uses [starts_at, ends_at) intervals.
BR-06 Enforce configurable minimum rest between adjacent shifts, including overnight shifts.
BR-07 Enforce employee eligibility/qualification for the incoming assignment.
BR-08 At most one active swap request may involve an assignment at a time.
BR-09 Revalidate ownership, timings and policy at approval in a transaction protected against race conditions.
BR-10 Only the employee assigned to the outgoing shift may author or submit its handover; supervisor administrative correction, if introduced, requires a separate audited permission.
BR-11 Only the employee owning the designated recipient shift at acknowledgement may acknowledge the submitted handover.
BR-12 Log swap decisions, assignment ownership changes, and handover transitions with actor and timestamp.

10. MVP IN-SCOPE / OUT-OF-SCOPE
10.1 IN SCOPE (v1.0)
- Authentication and basic role management for Employee, Supervisor, Admin.
- Departments, shift definitions and assignment calendar.
- Two-way shift swap proposal, peer decision, supervisor approval, automatic conflict checks.
- Handover drafts, submissions, acknowledgement and task status.
- Audit logs and minimal configurable policies.
10.2 OUT OF SCOPE (v1.1+)
- Automatic shift optimization, open-shift claims, attendance/GPS, payroll, leave management.
- WhatsApp/email and real-time push notifications.
- Advanced analytics, staffing forecasts, AI recommendations, attachments, multi-tenant billing.

11. PROPOSED TECHNOLOGY STACK
- Frontend: React, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS, FullCalendar.
- Backend: Laravel REST API, Laravel Sanctum.
- Database: PostgreSQL; transactional updates and indexed relationships.
- Tests: Pest (backend), Playwright (end-to-end).
- Development: Git, GitHub, Docker Compose.
These are design choices, not installed or implemented components.

12. MVP ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA
AC-01 Admin can create an employee, assign a role, and place the employee in a department.
AC-02 Supervisor can publish two non-overlapping assignments within their department.
AC-03 Employee sees their own scheduled assignments.
AC-04 Employee A can request exchange of two eligible future assignments with Employee B.
AC-05 Employee B can accept or reject; a rejection never changes either assignment.
AC-06 Only authorized supervisor can finally approve; approved exchange updates both owners together.
AC-07 An overlapping or rest-violating swap is rejected without modifying the schedule.
AC-08 Two concurrent approvals cannot cause a double swap or partial state.
AC-09 Outgoing employee can submit a handover to a designated recipient shift.
AC-10 Recipient employee can acknowledge, but unrelated employees cannot.
AC-11 Unfinished tasks remain open after acknowledgement and can later be updated with audit history.
AC-12 Key actions have traceable actor, timestamp and outcome in audit history.

13. OPEN DECISIONS TO FINALIZE BEFORE IMPLEMENTATION
OD-01 Organizational policy defaults: shift length, notice period, min rest and expiry duration.
OD-02 Whether shifts require position-specific qualifications; define matching data model.
OD-03 Whether supervisors may oversee more than one department and whether an employee may belong to multiple departments.
OD-04 How outgoing and incoming assignments are paired when shifts overlap or there is a gap.
OD-05 Correction policy for submitted handovers and task edit permissions.
OD-06 Conflict behavior when a swap changes a handover author or recipient before submission/acknowledgement.
OD-07 Precise statuses for a failed approval validation versus expiry/rejection.
OD-08 Timezone scope: one timezone per organization for MVP.

14. IMPLEMENTATION STATUS
Requirements drafted for Stage 1. No source code, database, test run or deployment is claimed. Stage 2 design must resolve the open decisions before schema migrations are frozen.
